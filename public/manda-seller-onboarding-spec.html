1. What This Is

manda is a platform (operated by iwoca, a UK SMB lender) that connects business sellers with buyers. This spec covers the digital onboarding journey that takes a UK SME owner from first contact to deal-ready status, without requiring deal team involvement until after qualification.

Why digital onboarding is needed:

Compliance. manda needs to meet KYC/KYB standards for new-to-iwoca customers (sellers who don't have an existing iwoca lending account). Today there is no digital path to do this.

Structured data. Data collected must feed into existing databases. Manual collection via email doesn't produce structured, system-ready data.

Highest friction stages automated. KYC/KYB, document collection/verification, and engagement letter signing are where sellers stall and deal team spends most time chasing. These currently account for the biggest pipeline drop-offs (engagement letter: 49% drop, documents: 46% drop).

Design principle: get to deal-ready as fast as possible without manual intervention or losing the seller along the way.

2. Journey Steps

Each step below is exactly what the prototype implements. The order is fixed - branching is documented within each step.

Step 1: Email

ScreenEmail input, single field
What happensSeller enters email. System sends a 6-digit OTP. Seller enters OTP to verify.
WhyEmail is the session key - allows resume later without passwords. OTP verifies ownership (prevents someone entering a competitor's email). No passwords to manage.
LogicOn verified OTP: check if email exists in LEGAL_ENTITY_EMAIL (iwoca's core customer database). If match → existing customer path. If no match → new customer path.
Data storedemail (session key)
Existing vs new customer: The journey is the same for both. The only difference is where pre-filled data comes from (iwoca's lending database vs Companies House) and that existing customers may start with an additional verified source for the confidence model (MCAT - iwoca's Manual Credit Assessment Tool, where analysts manually verify company financials during underwriting) if MCAT data is available for that customer. The split is determined automatically by email match, not a user-facing choice. The toggle in the prototype is for demo purposes only.

Step 2: Company Identification

Screen (existing)Company picker - shows all businesses linked to seller's iwoca account. Seller taps the one they're selling, then confirms revenue range (same dropdown as new customers). Revenue range is seller-declared because iwoca data (MCAT) may be stale for a growing company.
Screen (new)Type-ahead search against Companies House API. On select: show revenue field (select range) + PSC list + DOB confirmation.
Why (existing)Pre-fills everything. Reduces friction to one tap. Shows the seller "we already know you" - trust signal.
Why (new)Companies House search gives CRN, status, PSC, filings, address, SIC code in one API call. Revenue range needed for eligibility gate (£500k minimum). PSC + DOB = identity verification for v1 (no IDVerse).
Logic (new) 1. Companies House search → fetch company profile
2. Show PSCs from Companies House PSC API
3. Seller selects which PSC they are
4. If "I'm not listed here" → ineligible (only PSCs can authorise a sale)
5. If PSC shareholding band < 50% → ineligible (cannot authorise sale of majority voting shares)
6. If revenue band = "Under £500k" → ineligible
7. Seller enters full DOB → verified against protected Companies House register
Data storedcompany_number, company_name, psc_name, psc_dob, revenue_band
Decision: No IDVerse/Onfido for v1. PSC selection from Companies House + DOB verification + accounting software connect + Declaration of Authority clause in engagement letter is sufficient. IDVerse adds friction that kills conversion. If someone lies about being a PSC, the engagement letter is void under Fraud Act 2006 (section 12 of the letter).

WhyFail fast. Don't waste seller's time if they can't proceed. Don't waste deal team's time on ineligible companies.

Step 4: Eligible - Celebration

Screen"Your business is eligible for the manda platform." + company name pill + "Let's get you deal-ready" CTA.
WhyCreates momentum. The seller has cleared a gate - they feel invested. Celebrating eligibility creates a micro-commitment: they've passed a filter, which makes the next steps feel earned rather than demanded.
LogicOn CTA click → navigate to Personal Details, then Hub.

Step 5: Personal Details

ScreenFull name (read-only, from PSC register), email (read-only, from step 1), DOB (read-only, from step 2), phone (editable).
WhyShows the seller their identity is verified. Collects phone (needed for deal team contact). Minimal input - only phone is editable.
Logic (existing)Pre-fill from LEGAL_ENTITY_PERSON (name table) + LEGAL_ENTITY_PHONE (phone table) in iwoca DB.
Logic (new)Name from PSC API. Email from step 1. DOB from step 2. Phone = blank.
Data storedfull_name, email, dob, phone

Step 6: Hub (Checklist)

ScreenThree items: Business details (~3 min), Documents (Optional, ~5 min), Deal details (~3 min). Completable in any order.
WhyHub-spoke model. Gives seller control over order. Shows progress. "Optional" on documents reduces friction - seller doesn't feel blocked.
LogicRequires: Business details + Deal details completed. Documents is optional. When both required items are done → auto-navigate to Review.
Decision: Documents marked "Optional". Statutory accounts are already available from Companies House. Additional documents improve confidence but aren't blocking. They'll be collected before buyer introductions (post-qualification), not before engagement letter.

Description: Goes on buyer-facing listing. Can't be pre-filled.

ScreenThree sections: Already available, Connect your data, Upload documents.

Each connect/upload adjusts the confidence model (see Valuation section).

Step 9: Deal Details (spoke)

Fields Revenue (pre-filled, editable)
Reason for selling (dropdown)
Timeline (dropdown)
Asking price (empty, required)
How did you arrive at this price? (dropdown)
Post-sale involvement (dropdown)
Why each field Revenue: Pre-filled from MCAT/XBRL. Editable with override - but dropping to Low confidence discourages frivolous edits.
Reason for selling: Deal intelligence. Buyers want to know motivation. "Partner dispute" or "Health" signals urgency.
Timeline: Prioritisation. ASAP sellers get faster deal team response.
Asking price: Empty, not pre-filled. Framed as "What's the minimum you'd accept?" - anchors low, creates psychological commitment that's hard to backtrack from later.
How did you arrive at this price?: Deal intelligence. Tells the deal team whether the seller has a grounded expectation or needs guidance. No blocking - "Unsure" sellers benefit most from seeing the valuation estimate.
Post-sale involvement: Matching criterion. Some buyers want a clean break, others want the founder to stay.
Pre-fillRevenue from most recent source: MCAT (existing) or XBRL (new). All other fields = seller input.
Decision: EBITDA is never asked. Most SME owners don't know their EBITDA. It's derived server-side from XBRL (OperatingProfitLoss + Depreciation + Amortisation), MCAT, or accounting software. If unavailable from any source, valuation uses EV/Revenue multiple instead.
Decision: Asking price is required but empty. Prevents sellers from increasing their price later (a real problem - one seller recently found out he was popular and increased his asking price, delaying deal closure). "Minimum you'd accept" framing makes it psychologically hard to go above this number later.

Step 10: Review

ScreenSummary of all submitted data: Company (name, sector, headcount, registered address, website if provided), Financials (revenue), Deal (asking price, timeline, reason).
WhyStandard review step. Lets seller catch errors before signing. Creates a sense of finality - "this is going on record."
LogicOn confirm → proceed to Sign.

Step 11: Engagement Letter

ScreenScrollable engagement agreement (HTML rendered, not PDF). Sign area below: type name or draw signature. "Adopt signature and sign" button.
Agreement clauses12 sections - see Section 5 for full breakdown. Key: Services, Fees (tiered success fee), Anti-circumvention (18 months), Confidentiality (3 years), Declaration of Authority (Fraud Act 2006 deterrent).
Why here (not earlier)Two reasons: (1) Reward - the valuation estimate sits behind the signature. "Sign to unlock your valuation estimate" gives sellers a clear reason to sign now. (2) Legal protection - manda needs the agreement signed before sharing market intelligence (valuation data, sector multiples). Current funnel sends the letter cold and gets 49% drop because there's no incentive. After 10 minutes of onboarding, the seller has invested effort and wants the payoff.
Logic Template is hardcoded (not negotiable in digital onboarding).
On sign: record signatory_name, signature_image (base64 if drawn), signed_at (timestamp), ip_address.
After sign → loading screen "Calculating your valuation" → valuation reveal.
Data storedengagement_letter_signed_at, signatory_name, signature_type (typed/drawn), signature_data

Step 12: Valuation Estimate

ScreenConfetti animation. "Your valuation estimate" heading. Dark card showing Low / Mid / High range. Confidence badge. Disclaimer. "What happens next" box. "Schedule a call" CTA.
WhyThe reward. In practice, some sellers just want a valuation and aren't ready to sell immediately. Placing it at the end means they complete the full onboarding to get it - building a relationship with manda even if they don't list today. They stay in the funnel and come back when ready.
What's shown Range: £Low - £Mid - £High
Confidence badge (language only: "Medium confidence")
"Based on comparable transactions in your sector"
Disclaimer: "This is an indicative estimate only. The final sale price will depend on market conditions, buyer appetite, and the outcome of due diligence."
What's NOT shownThe multiple used. The formula. The EBITDA figure. The comp set. None of this is revealed to sellers - keeps methodology opaque, prevents sellers arguing with components.

3. MVP Scope

3.1 Tech stack

New FastAPI service (same stack as CCA [Credit Card Application service]: PostgreSQL + SQLAlchemy + Alembic + FastAPI). Reads from iwoca core Django DB (LEGAL_ENTITY tables, MCAT data) via internal API. Reuses existing patterns for document signing, eligibility, and open banking from the credit card service. Accounting software integrations (Xero/FreeAgent/Sage) are net-new infrastructure - no existing iwoca integration is reusable (see Section 3.2).

6. Valuation Calculation

6.1 Formula

Mid = EBITDA × Sector EV/EBITDA multiple
Low = Mid × (1 - spread)
High = Mid × (1 + spread)
Where spread = ±20%, ±35%, or ±40% depending on source confidence (see Section 6.5).

If EBITDA unavailable from any source:
Mid = Revenue × Sector EV/Revenue multiple
Then same range calculation above.

Output is Enterprise Value (total transaction value). This is what manda's fee is based on (Aggregate Consideration). Debt/equity split is a deal structure question resolved during DD, not at estimate stage.

6.2 EBITDA Derivation

EBITDA is never collected from the seller. It's derived from the most recent available source:

SourceHow EBITDA is calculatedAvailable for
Accounting software (Xero/FreeAgent/Sage)P&L report: Operating Profit + Depreciation + AmortisationSellers who connect
MCAT (Manual Credit Assessment)MCAT_OPERATING_PROFIT + MCAT_DEPRECIATION + MCAT_AMORTISATIONExisting iwoca customers whose financials were analyst-verified during lending underwriting
Management accounts (uploaded)Extracted from P&L in uploaded documentSellers who upload
XBRL (Companies House)OperatingProfitLoss + TangibleFixedAssetsDepreciationChargedInPeriod + IntangibleFixedAssetsAmortisationChargedInPeriodAll companies filing full accounts

Use whichever is most recent by date. If none available → fall back to EV/Revenue multiple.

6.3 Sector Classification

MCAT available: Direct match to manda sector (no vote needed). MCAT's 31 categories map near-directly to manda's sector list. The ~6 that don't map cleanly (other, other_business_services, other_domestic_services) default to "Other" with low confidence.

No MCAT: Corroboration model - classify each source independently into a manda sector, then majority wins.

SourceWhat it tells youAvailability
SIC code description (Companies House)What was filed at incorporation/annual return. Often stale.Always - from Companies House API
One-line descriptionWhat the seller says they do right now. Current.Always - required field in Business Details
Company websiteWhat the business presents to the market. Current.Sometimes - optional field
Accounting software (revenue by category)What the business actually makes money from. Objective.Build version - sellers who connect

Decision logic: Majority agree → use that sector. Tie → tiebreaker by reliability: accounting > description > website > MCAT > SIC. Only 1 source → use it. No sources produce a mappable sector → default to "Other" with low confidence (widest valuation range ±40%).

Sector list: MCAT's 31 industry categories (renamed for readability) + any additional sectors where comps data shows materially different multiples. Where multiple SIC codes exist for a company, use the first listed (primary activity).

6.5 Valuation Range

The range displayed to sellers is driven by source corroboration - how many independent data sources agree on the financials. More sources agreeing = tighter range.

ScenarioSpreadLanguage shown
1+ verified sources±20%"Medium confidence"
0 verified sources±35%"Low to medium confidence"
Seller has overridden pre-filled values±40%"Low confidence (self-reported)"

Floor rule

The range is never tighter than ±20%, regardless of how many sources agree. This prevents the estimate looking like a formal valuation. manda is not a valuation firm - this is an indicative estimate only.

What counts as a "source"

SourceCounts as independent source?
iwoca MCAT dataYes - but only available for existing customers with analyst-verified financials (not all iwoca customers have MCAT data)
Accounting software connectedYes
Open banking connectedYes - also serves as identity verification + proof of active trading
XBRL (Companies House)Yes - but only available for companies filing full accounts (not micro-entities with abbreviated filings)
Seller override/inputNo - drops confidence
Management accounts (uploaded)Partial - counts as 0.5 (seller-prepared, unaudited)
Decision: Range is always wide enough that sellers can't quote it as a formal valuation. ±20% minimum floor regardless of corroboration. This prevents sellers telling buyers "manda valued my business at £2.5M." The language ("indicative estimate", "comparable transactions") + disclaimer reinforces this.
